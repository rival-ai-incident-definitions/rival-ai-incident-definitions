# Diagnostic Criteria

Many diagnoses in clinical medicine have more than one published set of criteria. A reliability study has two clinicians apply one set to the same patients; a criteria comparison applies several sets to the same patients and cross-tabulates the diagnoses.

## Test-retest reliability

The DSM-5 Field Trials ([Regier et al. 2013](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2012.12070999)) define reliability as "the degree to which the diagnosis of a mental disorder by one clinician would likely be replicated by a second clinician interviewing the same patient". Two clinicians, blind to any previous diagnosis, interviewed each patient on separate occasions. Of 23 diagnoses with precise estimates, 5 had an intraclass kappa in the very good range, 9 good, 6 questionable and 3 unacceptable (Abstract). The DSM-IV field trials had instead estimated prevalence under DSM-III, DSM-III-R, ICD-10 and the proposed DSM-IV criteria (Previous Field Trials).

## Rival criteria on one cohort

Lam (2017, ch. 5) applied four newer Alzheimer's disease criteria to 155 patients who all met the older NINCDS-ADRDA criteria. Each newer set diagnosed 96% (DSM-5), 94% (NIA-AA), 55% (ICD-10) or 54% (IWG-1) of the cohort, and 53 patients (34%) met all five (p. 90). Counting possible AD as not AD, pairwise kappa among the newer sets ranged from 0.49 to 0.88 (Figure 5.1, p. 107). Lam concludes: "Even when significant overlap occurs, cases excluded by one criterion may be included by another." (p. 103)

[Dolci et al. (2017)](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2017.00203/full) classified 94 geriatric outpatients under DSM-IV with NINCDS-ADRDA and under NIA-AA: "Every patient who was classified as demented according to the DSM-IV criteria was also classified as demented according to the NIA-AA criteria" (p. 5). Of the 55 patients without dementia under the older criteria, 16 had possible AD under NIA-AA (Table 3, p. 4). Kappa for the three-level diagnosis was 70% (p. 5).

## Bearing on this study

**Crosswalk.** The study's [Crosswalk](/rival-ai-incident-definitions/crosswalk/) applies each definition to the same 214 incidents and reports both cells where two definitions part. Dolci's table leaves one of those cells empty. Lam's cohort was selected by one of the criteria compared, as the study's incidents are all admitted by the [AI Incident Database](/rival-ai-incident-definitions/definitions/aiid/) definition.

**Verdicts.** Crosswalk H4 uses the two-clinician design as a baseline: it tests whether two definitions disagree more than two CSET annotators applying one ([Verdicts](/rival-ai-incident-definitions/framework/verdicts/)).

**Three-state labels.** Where Lam resolved the possible grade by rule, the study keeps a can't-tell label and bounds each result over its resolutions ([Three-State Labels](/rival-ai-incident-definitions/framework/three-state/)).
