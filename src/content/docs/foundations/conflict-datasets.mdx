# Conflict Event Datasets

Political-violence research counts events with public datasets, each with its own definition of a conflict event. [Raleigh, Kishi and Linke (2023)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-023-01559-4) compared four global datasets, two automated (ICEWS, GDELT) and two researcher-led (ACLED, UCDP-GED), and conclude (Abstract) that

> answers to “which country is most violent?”; “where are civilians most at risk?”; and “is the frequency of conflict increasing or decreasing?” vary according to datasets all purporting to capture the same phenomena of political violence

The authors created ACLED (Competing interests).

## Inclusion thresholds

UCDP-GED admits an event only within a dyad that has a stated incompatibility and reaches 25 battle-related deaths a year, and only if the event causes at least one direct death. ACLED admits any act of political violence or demonstration involving an armed, organized group (p. 7). For Mexico in 2021, ACLED records 6739 civilian fatalities, over 81% of the violent fatalities it records there; UCDP-GED records 28, or 0.15% of its own (p. 2). For the Philippines in 2020, ACLED records at least 901 civilian fatalities and UCDP-GED 37 (pp. 8–9).

## Excluded events

At least four mayors assassinated in Madagascar in 2020 are absent from UCDP-GED because they fall outside its fatality criteria. "Persistent low-level political violence is entirely missing in some datasets, for example, because of definition restriction rather than its occurrence being in doubt" (p. 8).

## Mapping between schemes

For Figure 1 the authors mapped ACLED events onto UCDP-GED's three categories using ACLED's interaction codes (p. 9). Most of the comparison is by country, year and category, and the datasets also differ in sources and languages (pp. 10–11), so a gap in counts combines definition and sourcing. The authors write: "Acknowledging a prioritization of specific conflict forms, and privileging validity or reliability, are all acceptable choices." (p. 14)

## Bearing on this study

**Rules.** The UCDP-GED threshold is one clause that removes events before any analysis. The study writes each clause of an AI definition as a separate [rule](/rival-ai-incident-definitions/framework/rules/), so each clause's effect on the count is visible.

**Crosswalk.** The study holds the incidents and their codes fixed, 214 AI Incident Database incidents coded by CSET annotators, and varies only the definition, so a disagreement in its [Crosswalk](/rival-ai-incident-definitions/crosswalk/) is a disagreement between definitions.

**Verdicts.** The authors, who favor external validity, accept either priority. The study's [Verdicts](/rival-ai-incident-definitions/framework/verdicts/) report where definitions part without naming one correct.
