# Research Classification

Bibliometrics assigns articles to disciplines through journal classification schemes, of which several are in use. [Sīle et al. (2021)](https://direct.mit.edu/qss/article-pdf/2/1/65/1906502/qss_a_00110.pdf) classified peer-reviewed social-science and humanities articles from 2006–2015 in the national databases of Flanders (VABB-SHW) and Norway (Cristin) under five schemes: two local (VABB, NPU) and three international (Web of Science, Science-Metrix, ERIH PLUS).

## Common frame and pairwise design

The schemes differ in their categories and levels, so each was mapped by a crosswalk onto the OECD Fields of Research and Development classification (p. 71). Seven pairs were compared: each local scheme against each international scheme, and the two local schemes against each other (p. 75). Agreement on an article's set of disciplines was measured with the Jaccard index. Mean Jaccard across the pairs was 0.73, from 0.63 for NPU and Science-Metrix to 0.80 for NPU and VABB (p. 76).

## Counts and shares

The authors find that "the choice of disciplinary classification can lead to over- or underestimation of the absolute number of publications per discipline" and that "if the focus is on the relative numbers, the choice of classification has practically no influence" (Abstract, p. 65). The total count of social-science and humanities articles differed by up to 16%, for NPU against Science-Metrix in Norway (p. 85). The residual category *Other social sciences* differed by 670% between VABB and Web of Science (p. 81). The mean difference in a discipline's share was 1.6 percentage points (p. 81).

## Baseline and accuracy

The authors treat the two local schemes as the baseline for percentage error (pp. 70, 75), and cite earlier work for the point that "Agreement, however, does not mean that the classification is accurate." (p. 68)

## Bearing on this study

**Crosswalk.** As in Sīle et al., the study's [Crosswalk](/rival-ai-incident-definitions/crosswalk/) compares schemes pair by pair on the same cases: AI-incident definitions on 214 incidents, with the coded fields of each incident as the common frame.

**Verdicts.** The choice of scheme moved counts far more than shares, so whether a result holds depends on which statistic it names. Each registered hypothesis names its statistic and threshold, and its [Verdict](/rival-ai-incident-definitions/framework/verdicts/) is computed on that statistic.

**Definitions.** Sīle et al. chose a baseline scheme. The study names none of the four [definitions](/rival-ai-incident-definitions/definitions/) as correct, and reports agreement between them without reading it as accuracy.
